# Request art

After the first or second edit, the editor tells the art team what the piece needs. In the tracker that is an **art request**: one per piece, with one line for each image wanted.

## Make the request

1. Open the piece and go to its **Art request**.
2. Write the overview: what the piece is about, the lede ideas, anything the art team should know.
3. Add one **item** per image: a subject, an **image type** (*Photo*, *Portrait*, *Graphic or figure*, *Illustration*, *Animation*, *Video still*) and any notes.

<span class="screenshot-placeholder">Screenshot to add: an art request with three items</span>

The request appears on the art team's [Art requests](https://airtable.com/appFlJWmSk0RDIf1U/pbdMlP776N9XQeREM/pagcK8rOZFtdfgdEn){target=_blank} screen. As images come in and are approved, the counts — requested, received, approved — roll up to the request and to the piece, so an editor can see at a glance where the art stands without asking.

!!! tip "Asking for a graphic"
    Add an item of type **Graphic or figure** and the tracker ticks **Graphics needed** on the piece for you, which adds the *Commission infographic* step and tells the art editor and the graphics designer. See [Graphics and infographics](graphics-and-infographics.md).

## The lede

The lede illustration has its own chain of steps on the **Lede art** track — concept, assign artist, art agreement, creative brief, final, invoice — owned by the art editor and dated from the publish date. You don't request the lede; it is part of every article.
