# How it fits together

The tracker is built on four central ideas. This page describes them in outline.

## A piece

A **piece** is one thing Quanta will publish: a feature, a Q&A, an episode of *The Joy of Why*, a video, a newsletter. Each piece has a short working name (its slug, in capitals, like `RNA WORLD`), a **content type**, a **publish date** once it has one, and the people working on it.

A piece starts life as a **pitch**. At that stage it has no date and sits in the backlog. The moment someone gives it a publish date, it moves to the calendar and the tracker builds its to-do list.

## Steps

That to-do list is made of **steps**. A step is one job on one piece: *Fact check* on `RNA WORLD`, *Lede: creative brief* on `SPIDER SILK CONDENSATES`. Every step has an **owner**, a **due date**, often a **due back** date (when the work comes back to the editor), and a **Done** box.

You don't create the standard steps yourself. The tracker knows, for each content type, which steps a piece needs and how far before publication each one falls. An article gets about thirty; a podcast episode about sixteen. When the publish date moves, the dates move with it. When a step is yours, it is on your list.

!!! example "A worked example"
    Monday 14 December is set as the publish date for an article. The tracker immediately creates its steps: *Fact check* and *Top edit* due Tuesday 1 December (nine working days before), back Tuesday 8 December; *Copyedit* due Thursday 10 December, back Friday 11 December; *Final check* and *Publish* on the day; the social and archive steps after. Each lands on the right person's list.

## Tracks

Steps belong to **tracks**, which are the teams' lanes through a piece: **Editorial**, **Lede art**, **Secondary art**, **Graphics**, **Production** and **Audience**. Each track has its own status on the piece, so an editor can see at a glance that the lede is at *art agreement* while the text is at *copyedit*. A piece's overall **Status** is read from these: *Pitched* until it has a date, *Commissioned* once it does, then the latest editorial step, then *Published*. An editor can override it to *On hold* or *Killed*.

## People

Each piece names its people: **Editor**, **Hosts** (podcasts), **Top editor**, **Fact checker**, **Copy editor**, **Image researcher**, **Staging and publishing**, **Graphics designer**. The tracker uses these to decide who owns each step. Steps with no named person on the piece go to the team's default — the art editor for art steps, the production editor for staging, the audience team for social.

Change a person on the piece and their open steps follow them. That is the one habit to learn: to hand over a whole role, change it on the piece, not step by step.

## What the tracker does on its own

Four things happen without anyone asking. When a piece gets or changes its publish date, its steps are created or re-dated. When you tick a step, the piece's status updates. Every weekday morning at 8:30 New York, Slack sends you a list of what is due. And when a step lands on your list — new, or passed to you — Slack tells you.
